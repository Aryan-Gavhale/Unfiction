# PART A — THE SCRIPT

## How this script hooks people

The story is told like a heist film, not like a news report. These are the devices it uses, so you know what each line is doing when you perform it.

| Device | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Start inside the crime** | L01–L02 | The first words put the viewer at an ATM, mid-robbery, in the present tense. No intro, no "hi guys". |
| **A strange human detail first** | L03 | "They were told they were film extras." People stay for people, not for numbers. |
| **Scale reveal** | L04–L05 | One man becomes 28 countries and ₹94 crore. The jump in scale is the hook. |
| **A mystery the viewer wants solved** | L07–L08 | The bank looked and saw nothing. *How?* That question carries all of Act 1. |
| **Dramatic irony** | L09 | The FBI warned banks the day before. The viewer now knows more than the bank did. |
| **A promise with a payoff** | L11 | "One man was caught, and his court papers name the bank." Paid off at L66–L68. |
| **Open loops** | L21–L22, L27–L28, L46 | "Keep SWIFT in mind, it comes back Monday." "Remember Big Boss." "The robbers weren't finished." Each loop closes later. |
| **Act-out cliffhangers** | End of every act | Each act ends on a question or a turn, never on a summary. |
| **Pattern interrupts** | Every 45–60 seconds | A silence, a hard cut, a slap, a clock stopping. Attention resets. |
| **Callbacks** | L22, L49, L69, L78, L80 | Episode 1 links, plus the final return to the ATM from L01. Makes the series feel connected. |
| **The viewer as judge** | L79 | The closing question asks for an opinion, not a fact, which drives comments. |

**Open loops and where they close:**

| Loop opened | Closed |
|---|---|
| "Told they were film extras" (L03) | L31, and the final line at L78 |

## Shot legend

| Tag | What it means |
|---|---|
| `ME` | You on camera. Every `ME` line also has a **Faceless** alternative, in case you skip the camera for that line or for the whole episode. |
| `EVID` | A real official document on screen (US government alert, court record, DOJ release). Used for four beats only. Highlight the key line as you read it. All four are US government documents, which are public domain. |
| `CARD` | Your own designed text card that quotes or summarises a source, with the source named at the bottom. |
| `GFX` | 2D motion graphics from the Unfiction toolkit: maps, clocks, flow diagrams, counters, timelines. |
| `ANIM` | 3D Blender scenes: faceless characters, props and environments. Small **"DRAMATISATION"** label in the corner. |

**Rough shot split (by narration time):** GFX about 31%, ME about 29%, ANIM about 19%, CARD about 14%, EVID about 7%. If you go faceless, the ME share moves into GFX and ANIM.

**Stock footage:** none. **Real CCTV:** none. Every "CCTV" shot is your own Blender render, labelled as a dramatisation.

**Do not use:** news channel footage, the real Cosmos Bank logo, Visa or RuPay logos, photos of any accused person, or music from films. Bank and card network names in text are fine.

## Say it, show it: the one rule for every line

Every line in this script has a visual that shows **exactly** what the words say, at the moment they say it. If the narration says "ninety-five cloned cards", the screen shows cards, and the number 95. If it says "half past two in the morning in Los Angeles", a clock shows 02:30 with a moon. This is what makes a viewer feel the story instead of just hearing it.

Three working rules:

1. **The key word lands on a visual hit.** The toolkit's cue sheet (`cues.py`) gives you the exact second each number, highlight or stamp appears. Line the spoken word up with that frame.
2. **Change the picture every 3 to 6 seconds.** On long `ME` lines, cut between your two framings (see [C5](#c5-sitting-angle-framing-and-eyeline)).
3. **Never show a visual the words don't support.** On maps, pin only countries that are named in sources (the US, UK, UAE, Russia and India). The other 23 countries were never published, so they get a counter, not invented dots.

## COLD OPEN — 00:00 to 02:36

*How to read this act: hushed, present tense, unhurried. You're describing something happening right now in front of you.*

**L01** · `ANIM` · A busy Kolhapur street in hard afternoon sun. A faceless young man in a plain shirt, with a backpack, walks into a small glass ATM booth. `TimeChip`: **SATURDAY · 11 AUGUST 2018 · KOLHAPUR**.
Sound: street ambience (scooters, a distant horn), the booth door. The first word is spoken within two seconds.
> Saturday afternoon, August 2018. Kolhapur, in Maharashtra. A young man walks into an ATM booth with a backpack and a stack of debit cards.

**L02** · `ANIM` · Close-up of the ATM. A card goes in. Screen: `PLEASE WAIT`, then `APPROVED`. The cash shutter opens and notes slide out. His hand takes them and immediately pushes in the next card.
Sound: keypad beeps, a one-second pause, then the **approval chime** (motif 1, first use), cash dispenser whirr.
> He puts in a card. The machine thinks for a second, and pays. He takes the cash and puts in the next card. Then the next one.

**L03** · `ME` · Medium close-up (MCU), single hard key light. Slow. Small pause after "film".
*Faceless:* overhead shot of your hands fanning out a stack of plain white cards on a dark desk.
Sound: clapperboard clack under "film" (motif 4), very quiet.
> Some of the men doing this that day had been told they were extras in a Bollywood film. Their scene was simple: walk up to a cash machine and take out money. At least, that's what they were told.

**L04** · `GFX` · `world-28`. The dark world map draws in. Pins pop one by one as each place is named: MOSCOW, LONDON, DUBAI, LOS ANGELES. Kolhapur pulses in India. Counter top right: **28 COUNTRIES**.
Sound: pin pops on each country name.
> And he isn't alone. That same afternoon, people are doing exactly the same thing in twenty-eight countries: in Russia, in Britain, in the Gulf, in the United States. All with cards from one bank.

**L05** · `GFX` · `stopwatch-total`. Black screen. A stopwatch races from 0:00:00 to **2:13:00** and freezes. Underneath, a counter climbs to **₹94.42 CRORE**, with "≈ $13.5 MILLION" below.
Sound: fast ticking, then a low bass hit on the freeze.
> Most of it will happen in just two hours and thirteen minutes. By the time it's over, the bank will have lost ninety-four crore rupees. About thirteen and a half million dollars.

**L06** · `ANIM` → `GFX` · The generic co-op bank HQ in Pune, afternoon. Cut inside: a quiet office, one faceless staffer at a monitor. `visa-alert` pops on the screen: "Unusual volume of withdrawals on your cards." (Label it RECREATION.)
Sound: weekend office room tone, one notification ping.
> The bank is Cosmos, a hundred-and-twelve-year-old co-operative bank in Pune. Late that afternoon, its staff get an urgent warning from Visa: huge numbers of withdrawals are being made on Cosmos cards.

**L07** · `ANIM` · Over the shoulder onto the staffer's monitor: a transactions dashboard with a flat line and "ABNORMAL: 0". The staffer leans back, puzzled.
Sound: music drops out. Room tone only. **Silence 0.8 s** after the last word.
> They check their own system. Everything looks normal. Not one suspicious transaction.

**L08** · `ME` · Lean in slightly. Tight framing (4K punch-in).
*Faceless:* `two-servers`. Two identical server icons side by side; one turns red and is stamped "FAKE".
Sound: intruder glitch (motif 3) on "fake copy".
> The system wasn't lying. It just wasn't being told. Somewhere inside the bank, hackers had built a fake copy of one of its most important computers. And that copy was saying "yes" to everyone.

**L09** · `CARD` · `fbi-alert`. "FBI ALERT · FRIDAY 10 AUGUST 2018" / *"…planning to conduct a global Automated Teller Machine (ATM) cash-out scheme in the coming days."* Source tag: *FBI confidential alert, as reported by KrebsOnSecurity, 12 Aug 2018.*

**L10** · `GFX` · `flash-cuts`, 0.5 seconds each: a film clapperboard / a dark forum post signed "BIG BOSS", blurred / a Hong Kong skyline / the North Korea night-map glow.

*Faceless:* a slow push-in on the `restitution` court document, blurred, with only the words "Cosmos Co-operative Bank" sharp.

## ACT 1 — THE FAKE BRAIN — 02:36 to 05:19

*How to read this act: the friendly explainer. You're teaching a smart friend how something works. Slightly brighter energy.*

**L13** · `ANIM` · Pune at dusk: low-poly buildings, hills behind, lit windows, traffic trails. The camera glides to a plain building with generic "CO-OPERATIVE BANK" signage.
> Cosmos Co-operative Bank was founded in Pune in 1906. By 2018, it was one of the biggest co-operative banks in India: the kind of local bank families stay with for generations.

**L14** · `ME` · MCU. Conversational, a small smile.
*Faceless:* the ATM close-up prop, a card hovering at the slot, frozen.
> To understand how it was robbed, you need to know what happens in the few seconds after you put your card into an ATM.

**L15** · `GFX` · `switch-explainer`, beat 1. Nodes appear left to right as each is named: CARD → ATM → **SWITCH** → **CORE BANKING ("YOUR BALANCE")**. A glowing particle travels the chain.
Sound: soft UI ticks as each node appears.
> When you ask for cash, the ATM sends a message to your bank. It first arrives at a computer called a switch. The switch asks the bank's main system one question: does this person have the money?

**L16** · `GFX` · `switch-explainer`, beat 2. The reply travels back. A green **APPROVED** tag reaches the ATM and a cash icon pops. A second request comes back with a red **DECLINED** tag.
Sound: approval chime (motif 1), then a low decline buzz.
> If the answer is yes, the switch sends back "approved", and the machine pays. If not, "declined". Every withdrawal on earth depends on that one quiet answer.

**L17** · `GFX` · `switch-explainer`, beat 3. A red-outlined box slides in beside SWITCH: **PROXY SWITCH**. The link from it to CORE BANKING turns grey and dashed. A group of red card icons routes into the proxy, and it fires "APPROVED" back again and again.
Sound: intruder glitch as the proxy appears; the approval chime repeats faster and slightly out of tune.
> Now imagine someone builds a second switch: a copy, sitting right next to the real one. Requests from certain cards never reach the main system at all. The fake switch answers them itself. And it always says yes.

**L18** · `CARD` · `proxy-quote`. *"During the malware attack, a proxy switch was created and all the fraudulent payment approvals were passed by the proxy switching system."* Highlight "a proxy switch was created". Source tag: *Cosmos Bank statement, via Reuters, August 2018.*
> That's what Cosmos said happened. In its own words: "a proxy switch was created, and all the fraudulent payment approvals were passed by the proxy switching system."

**L19** · `ME` · MCU. Slow down on the last sentence.
*Faceless:* `switch-explainer` held on the CORE BANKING node, now dimmed, with a small tag "QUIET SATURDAY".
> That's why the staff saw nothing. The bank's main system never heard about the withdrawals. As far as it knew, it was a quiet Saturday.

**L20** · `ANIM` · Reuse the EP01 office desk. A faceless employee clicks open an email. For one frame the screen flickers red.
Sound: mouse click, then the intruder glitch, very short.
> How did the hackers get inside? According to the BBC, the usual way: a phishing email that let malware into the bank's network.

**L21** · `GFX` · `phish-spread`. One red node spreads across the network. Two labelled targets light up as named: **ATM SWITCH**, then **SWIFT**.
> From there, they moved towards the two systems that actually move money: the ATM switch, and the bank's link to SWIFT, the network banks use to send money abroad.

**L22** · `ME` · MCU. Knowing tone, a slight raise of the eyebrows.

# PART C — PRODUCTION GUIDE

## C1. The end-to-end pipeline

This is the order that saves the most time. The big rule: **lock the script before you record anything, and record the voice before you build any timing-dependent graphics or animation.** Every hour of rework in editing comes from breaking that order.

| # | Stage | Output | Time budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Script lock** | This file, read aloud once, final | 2 h |
| 2 | **Table read and timing** | A rough phone recording of the whole script, to find tongue-twisters and check runtime | 1 h |
| 3 | **Voice-over recording** | Clean VO for every non-`ME` line, one file per act | 2–3 h |
| 4 | **On-camera shoot** | All 22 `ME` lines, two takes each (skip if faceless) | 2–3 h |
| 5 | **Voice cleanup** | Every clip through the same Voice Studio preset, normalised | 1 h |
| 6 | **Radio edit** | VO and `ME` audio laid in order on the timeline, no visuals yet. This *is* your final timing. | 2 h |
| 7 | **Graphics** | ~45 toolkit scenes, beats fitted to the radio edit, exported | 8–10 h |
| 8 | **Blender** | ~12 ANIM shots, rendered to the radio-edit timing | 8–12 h (mostly unattended render time) |
| 9 | **Picture edit** | Visuals laid over the radio edit | 4–5 h |
| 10 | **Sound** | Music, SFX, ducking, loudness | 3–4 h |
| 11 | **Colour, captions, review passes** | Final picture and SRT | 2–3 h |
| 12 | **Packaging** | Thumbnail (two versions), title, description, chapters | 2 h |
| 13 | **Upload and publish** | Scheduled video, end screen, cards, pinned comment | 1 h |

**Total:** about 40–50 hours, much of it render time. With a day job, that's a two-week cycle:

| Day | Work |
|---|---|
| Day 1 (evening) | Script read-through, table read, mark tricky lines |
| Day 2 (evening) | Record all VO |
| Day 3 (weekend morning) | Shoot all `ME` lines (light is most stable, house is quiet) |
| Day 3 (afternoon) | Voice cleanup + radio edit |
| Days 4–7 | Graphics (6–8 scenes per evening). Start Blender renders overnight. |
| Days 8–9 | Remaining renders, picture edit |
| Day 10 (weekend) | Sound, colour, captions, review passes |
| Day 11 | Thumbnail, title, description, upload as private |
| Day 12 | Final watch on phone, fix list, publish |

## C2. Before you shoot

1. **Read the whole script aloud once, standing up, at full performance energy.** Mark any sentence you trip on. If you trip on it twice, rewrite it shorter. Do this before anything else.
2. **Record a table read on your phone.** It doesn't have to be good. Its job is to tell you the real runtime (target 19–20 minutes) and which lines run long.
3. **Make two scripts from this one:**
   - a **VO script** with only the non-`ME` lines, grouped by act, and
   - a **camera script** with only the 22 `ME` lines, with the line number in big text before each line.
4. **Put the camera script into a teleprompter app** on a phone or tablet. Keep lines short (5–7 words per line), font large, scroll speed set to about 130 words per minute. Put the prompter as close under the lens as possible, so your eyes barely move.
5. **Prepare your set the night before** (C3) and charge everything: phone, mic, lights, power bank.
6. **Clear storage.** 4K 30 fps uses roughly 350–400 MB per minute. For two hours of takes, keep 60 GB free.

## C3. Your set and background

Build one set and keep it for every episode. A consistent set is part of the channel's look, and you never have to think about it again.

- **Distance:** sit at least **1.5–2 m in front of the background**. Distance makes the background fall into soft blur and lets the light on you stand out.
- **Background:** dark and uncluttered, with depth. Good options:
  - a bookshelf with a few objects, not packed;
  - a corkboard "evidence wall" with printed maps, red string and pins (it fits the channel perfectly, and you can change the printouts per episode: Pune map, ATM photo, "₹94.42 CR");
  - a plain dark wall with one **practical lamp** (a small lamp that's visible in frame, warm bulb) on a table behind you.
- **One colour accent:** an RGB LED strip or small tube behind you, set to the channel's **red** (or a deep teal for contrast). Point it at the wall, not at the camera. This single detail makes a home room look like a set.
- **Avoid:** bare white walls, windows behind you (you become a silhouette), mirrors, ceiling fans in frame (flicker and motion), visible logos, family photos, bedsheets.
- **Mark the floor.** Once the set looks right, put tape marks for your chair legs, tripod legs and light stands, and take a phone photo of the whole setup. Next episode takes 10 minutes to rebuild instead of an hour.

## C4. Camera settings

You don't need a new camera. A recent phone shoots better video than most beginner cameras, if you lock its settings. The default camera app keeps changing exposure and focus, which is what makes home video look amateur.

**App:** use **Blackmagic Camera** (free, iPhone and Android). It gives you manual control over everything below. Your phone's "Pro video" mode also works.

| Setting | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Resolution | **4K (3840×2160)** | You edit on a 1080p timeline, so 4K lets you crop in up to 2× for a second, tighter framing from the same take. |
| Frame rate | **30 fps** | Your toolkit exports graphics at 60 fps, which divides cleanly into 30. Keep the whole project at 30. |
| Shutter | **1/50** | India's mains power is 50 Hz. At 1/60, ordinary LED and tube lights flicker on video. 1/50 removes it. (1/50 is fine at 30 fps.) |
| ISO | **Lowest that exposes properly** (100–400) | Higher ISO adds grain and smear. Add light instead of ISO. |
| White balance | **Fixed**, e.g. 5600 K for daylight LEDs, 3200 K for warm bulbs | Auto white balance drifts during a take; your skin changes colour between cuts. |
| Focus | **Locked on your eyes** | Stops the phone hunting when you move your hands. |
| Exposure | **Locked** | Stops the brightness pumping. |
| Lens | **The 2× lens (or 1× from further back, then crop)** | The 1× lens is wide (about 24–26 mm) and distorts faces at close distance: big nose, small ears. The 2× lens (about 48–52 mm) is flattering. Place the phone about 1.2–1.5 m away. |
| HDR / Dolby Vision | **Off** | HDR clips look washed out or over-bright when edited in a normal timeline and uploaded. Shoot SDR. |
| Stabilisation | **Off** on a tripod | Stabilisation crops and can wobble on a locked shot. |
| Codec | HEVC / H.265 is fine | Make proxies if your PC struggles (C15). |
| Phone mode | **Airplane mode + Do Not Disturb** | A notification mid-take ruins it, and some phones drop frames when a call comes in. |

**Mount:** a tripod with a phone clamp, never handheld. Lens at **eye level** (see C5). If the phone heats up and dims the screen, take it out of its case and point a small fan at it between takes.

## C5. Sitting angle, framing and eyeline

### How to sit

- **Chair:** a fixed chair with no wheels and no swivel. Swivel chairs make you rock without noticing, which looks nervous on video.
- **Sit on the front half of the seat**, back straight, not touching the backrest. This lifts your chest, opens your voice and stops slouching.
- **Turn your body 20–30° away from the camera, then turn your head back to face the lens.** Square-on to the camera looks flat and wide, like a passport photo. The slight angle adds depth and looks more cinematic.
- **Lean in about 10° towards the camera.** It reads as engaged and confident. For hook lines (L08, L24, L35, L52), lean in a little more.
- **Shoulders down and relaxed.** Take one big breath and drop your shoulders before each take.
- **Hands:** rest them on the desk or on your knee, and let them move naturally in the lower part of the frame. Small gestures on key words are good. Don't fiddle with a pen.

### Camera height

- The lens should be at **eye level or 2–3 cm above**. Never below your chin: looking down at the camera adds a double chin and feels like a video call.

### Framing

Shoot one wide-ish framing in 4K and crop the second one in the edit.

| Framing | What's in frame | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| **MCU (medium close-up)**, the 4K frame | Mid-chest to just above your head | Most `ME` lines: explaining, transitions |
| **CU (close-up)**, a 1.5–2× crop in the edit | Chin to forehead, top of head slightly cut | Hooks, reveals, act endings: L08, L24, L28, L35, L46, L52, L60, L71, L76 |

- **Position:** put yourself on the **left third** of the frame, with your eyes on the upper third line. The right side stays clear for source tags, names and callouts, the same as EP01.
- **Headroom:** small. In the MCU, leave about a hand's width above your head. In the CU, it's fine to cut the top of your head.
- **On long `ME` lines** (L11, L31, L60), cut from MCU to CU halfway, on a full stop. It feels like a second camera, and it hides any small stumble.

### Eyeline

- **Look into the lens, not at the screen.** The lens is the viewer's eyes.
- If you use a teleprompter, put it **directly under the lens** and sit far enough back that your eyes don't visibly scan left to right.
- For a thinking moment (L31, L40), you can glance briefly down and away, then come back to the lens for the key sentence.

## C6. Lighting

Lighting matters more than the camera. The EP01 script asked for a **single hard light from one side**, which is the channel's look: serious, investigative, a little cinematic. Here are three setups, from free to full.

**Before any setup:** **turn off the overhead light.** Ceiling light makes dark shadows under your eyes and nose and can flicker. All your light should come from the side and front.

### Setup A: free (window light)

- Sit with a window **to one side of you**, at about 45° in front. Never behind you, never directly in front.
- Shoot when the light is indirect: no direct sun falling on you. Overcast days are perfect.
- For more drama, close the curtain on the other windows so the far side of your face falls into shadow.
- For softer, put a white bedsheet or a sheet of white thermocol (foam board) on your shadow side, just out of frame, to bounce a little light back.
- **Problem:** window light changes every few minutes, so shoot all `ME` lines in one block, in the morning.

### Setup B: one light (the channel look, recommended)

- **One LED light**: a COB light (60–100 W) with a softbox or umbrella, or a large LED panel.
- Place it **45° to one side and about 45° in front of you**, slightly **above eye level**, angled down about 30°.
- **No fill light.** Let the far side of your face fall into soft shadow. You should see a small triangle of light on your shadow-side cheek under the eye; that's the classic "Rembrandt" look.
- **Hard vs soft:** with the softbox off, the light is harder and more dramatic (like EP01's cold open). With the softbox on, it's gentler. Use the softbox for most lines; for the cold open lines (L03, L08, L11), you can take it off for a harder look, as long as you shoot all of them in one go.
- Add the **practical lamp** and the **red accent strip** from C3 behind you. That gives three levels of light: you, the warm lamp, the coloured wall.

### Setup C: three lights (full)

| Light | Position | Power |
|---|---|---|
| **Key** | 45° to one side, slightly above eye level | Full (sets your exposure) |
| **Fill** | Opposite side, low, near the camera | About a quarter of the key, or a white reflector instead |
| **Rim / hair light** | Behind you, on the opposite side to the key, high, pointing at the back of your head and shoulder | Low; just enough to draw a thin bright edge that separates you from the dark background |

### Lighting rules

- **All lights at the same colour temperature** (for example all 5600 K). The only exception is the warm practical lamp, which is meant to look warm.
- **Expose for your face.** The bright cheek should be bright but never pure white. Lock the exposure there.
- **Glasses:** raise the key light higher and further to the side until the reflection leaves your lenses, or tilt the glasses down very slightly.
- **For the final lines** (L79–L80): make the scene feel a touch warmer. Either lower the key's colour temperature by about 300–500 K, or warm it slightly in the edit. EP01 did the same, so it's a channel habit: the story ends, and you relax.
- **Write your settings down:** light positions, power percentage, colour temperature, camera settings. Take a photo. Next episode, you match it exactly.

## C7. Wardrobe and grooming

- **Colour:** dark solid colours: black, charcoal, navy, olive, deep maroon. They look serious and they match the channel.
- **Avoid:** fine stripes and small checks (they shimmer on video), pure white (it glows and loses detail), big logos and text, shiny fabrics.
- **Same outfit for every `ME` line of the episode.** Take a photo, because you may need to re-shoot one line later.
- **Consider a signature look**, the same style in every episode (for example a dark overshirt). Viewers start recognising you by it.
- **Shine:** a hard key light shows every bit of oil on your forehead and nose. Use oil-blotting paper or a small amount of translucent matte powder before each block of takes.
- **Hair:** set it once before shooting and don't touch it.
- **Before every take:** check your collar, the mic cable (hidden), and that nothing is in your teeth.

## C8. Delivery: how to perform on camera

You said your face and voice look unprofessional. Usually that comes down to **energy and lighting, not looks**. Good lighting (C6) fixes half of it; these habits fix the rest.

1. **Talk to one person.** Imagine one friend sitting behind the lens who doesn't know this story. Never "Hey everyone"; always "you".
2. **Add 20% energy.** The camera flattens you. What feels slightly too much in the room looks normal on video.
3. **Slow down on numbers and names.** "Ninety-four crore… forty-two lakh." "Ghaleb… Alaumary." Viewers need half a second to take in each one.
4. **Pause before the reveal, not after.** "And that copy was saying… *yes* to everyone." The pause creates the suspense.
5. **Match your face to the line.** Half a smile on the absurd bits (L40 "With a supervisor."); still and serious on the heavy ones (L71).
6. **End sentences down.** Indian English and nervous speakers often rise at the end of sentences, which sounds unsure. Let statements fall in pitch at the full stop.
7. **Don't memorise everything.** Use the teleprompter, or the **look-down method**: read one line silently, look up into the lens, say it. You'll cut the look-down out in the edit.
8. **Warm up for 3 minutes:** hum, lip trills, say "red leather, yellow leather" ten times, then read L01 aloud twice.
9. **Two takes per line:** the first one safe, the second one bolder. Pick the better one in the edit. Three at most; after that, you get worse.
10. **Say the line number before each take.** "L08, take 2." Then three seconds of silence, then the line. This makes searching and editing much faster (C15).

## C9. Shooting day: the fast way

**Order the shoot by framing and energy, not by line number.** Changing position or energy between every line is what makes a shoot slow.

| Block | Lines | Energy |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Explainer lines (MCU) | L14, L19, L22, L40, L49, L57, L62, L68 | Friendly, conversational |
| 2. Story and hook lines (MCU, cropped to CU later) | L03, L08, L11, L24, L28, L31, L35, L46, L52, L60, L71, L76 | Intense, lean in |
| 3. Warm close (change lighting slightly) | L79, L80 | Relaxed, warmer |
| 4. Faceless inserts (overhead) | Hands with cards, pointing at maps on the corkboard, writing "₹94.42 CR" in red marker | — |
| 5. Silent B-roll | 10 seconds each: you reading papers, looking at the board, writing notes | — |

**During the shoot:**

- **Record 30 seconds of room tone** at the start: total silence, same mic, same room. You'll need it to fill gaps in the edit.
- **Check the first take properly:** play it back with headphones. Check focus, exposure, audio level and framing. Then check one take every 10 lines.
- **Clap once on camera** at the start of each take if you're recording audio on a separate device. It gives you a sync point.
- **After any break, re-check focus and exposure lock.** Phones often reset them.
- **Keep water nearby.** Sip between blocks, not between every take (swallowing changes your voice).
- **Don't review every take.** Trust the plan, and pick takes in the edit.

Blocks 4 and 5 take 15 minutes and give you footage for faceless fallbacks, Shorts and cutaways for the next few episodes too.

## C10. Going faceless (fully or partly)

Every `ME` line in the script has a **Faceless** alternative written under it. You can choose per line or for the whole episode.

**Three ways to work:**

| Option | What it looks like | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Fully faceless** | Use the Faceless alternative on all 22 lines | If you're not comfortable on camera yet. The channel still works: the story and graphics carry it. |
| **Hands and desk** | Overhead or over-the-shoulder shots of your hands with cards, maps, documents and a red marker | A strong middle ground: human and personal, no face |
| **Hybrid (recommended to start)** | Face only on the **hook and reveal lines** (L03, L11, L24, L52, L60, L76, L79), Faceless for the rest | 7 lines instead of 22. You get the trust a face brings, with a much easier shoot. |

**Silhouette option:** you in the chair with only the rim light on and the key light off, face in shadow, red accent behind. It keeps the voice and the "presenter" feel, looks very cinematic for this genre, and hides the face.

**Whatever you choose, use your own voice.** As we discussed, an AI voice risks YouTube's "inauthentic content" rules and audience trust. A clean recording of your real voice, with good mic technique (C11), sounds professional.

## C11. Recording your voice

**One mic setup for everything.** EP01 had the face lines on the collar mic and the faceless lines on the table mic, which is why they sounded different. For EP02, record **all** VO and **all** `ME` lines with the **collar mic, in the same position, in the same room, at the same gain**. Then they match automatically.

### Mic placement

- Clip the collar mic **mid-chest, about a hand's width (15–20 cm) below your chin**, centred.
- Make sure it can't rub against clothing. Run the cable inside your shirt and tape a small loop near the mic so cable movement doesn't make noise.
- Same position every time: mark the button or seam you clip it to.
- For VO-only recording, sit in the same chair, at the same desk, in the same posture as for the camera lines.

### Recorder settings

- **WAV, 48 kHz, 24-bit** if your recorder or app allows it. (48 kHz matches video.)
- **Gain:** set it so your loudest lines peak at about **−12 to −6 dBFS**. Never let it hit 0; distortion can't be fixed.
- If you record into the phone while filming, set the audio input to the external mic and turn off any "voice enhancement" or "wind noise reduction" in the camera app.

### The room

- The smaller and softer the room, the better. A bedroom with a bed, curtains and a carpet beats a big empty hall.
- **Turn off the fan and AC** while recording (they create hum that cleanup tools struggle with). Take breaks to cool down.
- **Close the windows** and record at a quiet time (early morning or late night).
- If the room echoes, hang a thick blanket behind you, out of frame, and put cushions on hard surfaces.
- **Phone in airplane mode**, fridge off if it's close, ceiling fan off.

### Technique

- **Drink water** and avoid milk, tea and coffee for an hour before; they make the mouth sticky and clicky. A slice of green apple helps if you hear mouth clicks.
- **Record VO by act**, one file per act. Say the line number before each line ("L15"), pause two seconds, then read it. If you stumble, stay silent for two seconds and **restart the whole sentence**, never just the word.
- **Smile slightly on the friendly lines.** It's audible.
- **Read Act 3 standing**, if your setup allows. More energy, faster pace. But keep the same mic distance.
- **Record pickups the same day**, with the same mic, room and posture. A pickup recorded a week later sounds different.

### Cleanup chain

Run **every** clip (VO and `ME`) through the **same chain at the same settings**:

1. **Voice Studio** (`voice-tool/Voice Studio.bat`). Pick the settings once on a clip from Act 3 (the most energetic), save them as a preset called **EP02**, and use that preset on every file. Turn "match" on. Set the **target to −16 LUFS** for voice clips (your current setting is −14, which is the target for the *final mix*, not for voice alone).
2. If you use **Adobe Podcast Enhance**, use it at **50–70% on every clip, at the same strength**. Mixing enhanced and non-enhanced clips is what makes voices sound inconsistent.
3. In the editor (if not already done by Voice Studio): high-pass filter at 80 Hz, gentle compression (3:1), a de-esser if the "s" sounds are sharp.
4. **Every voice clip ends up at about −16 LUFS.** Music and effects take the final mix to −14 LUFS.

The EP01 `sound-design.md` (section 10) has the full detail on EQ per mic and level targets; it applies to EP02 unchanged.

## C12. Music and sound for EP02

### The sound of this episode

EP01 sounded like a cold office at night: printers, clocks, a single ping. EP02 is a **daytime, street-level heist that turns into a global machine**, so it sounds busier and more rhythmic: ATM beeps, a ticking clock, crowds. Keep the channel rules from EP01:

- Music is felt, never noticed. It sits **under** the voice (−30 to −24 dBFS while you talk).
- **Silence is a tool.** The six silence points below are as important as any music cue.
- No famous film music, no Bollywood songs (even though the story mentions Bollywood), nothing with lyrics.
- Local colour is welcome but **subtle**: a low tanpura drone or filtered tabla loop under the India scenes, never a sitar cliché on top.

### Five motifs

| # | Motif | Sound | Where it plays |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The approval chime** | A clean two-note ATM "approved" beep (rising) plus the cash whirr | L02 (first, clean) → L16 (explained) → L17 (faster, out of tune) → L37–L38 (overlapping, frantic) → L78 (one last, lonely chime) |
| 2 | **The 2:13 clock** | A dry, close clock tick | Starts right after L35 "it started", runs under the whole of Act 3, speeds up gradually, **stops dead at L45** |
| 3 | **Intruder glitch** (shared with EP01) | A short digital glitch | L08 "fake copy", L17 proxy switch, L20 phishing click, L61 FASTCash. Same sound as EP01, so the series has one "hacker" sound. |
| 4 | **The clapperboard** | A wooden clap | L03 under "film", L31 at the start, L78 on "they had". Ties the film-extra story together. |
| 5 | **Silence** | Nothing but room tone | See the silence points below |

**Making the approval chime:** don't record at a real ATM: guards object, and you'd capture other people. Use a library "ATM beep" or "terminal confirm" sound, or make one: two short sine tones, the second higher (for example 880 Hz then 1,320 Hz, 80 ms each). Keep that exact file for the whole episode.

## C13. Graphics workflow

1. **Set up the folder:** copy `toolkit/examples/episode-template.html` into `episodes/EP02-cosmos-bank-heist/gfx-scenes.html`. Set `title` and `slug: 'ep02'`.
2. **Add the Indian cities once** to `UF.geo.CITIES` in `toolkit/core/uf-geo.js`: PUNE, KOLHAPUR, INDORE, AJMER, BHIWANDI, AURANGABAD, HYDERABAD, GOA (coordinates in the graphics build list). Then every scene can use them by name.
3. **Build from the radio edit, not the script.** For each scene, play its VO, note the second each beat lands, and write those numbers in the scene's `B` object. You never retime a graphic in the editor.
4. **Copy before you build.** Many EP02 scenes are close to EP01 scenes: `world-28` from the money trail map, `clocks-five` from the EP01 clocks, `phish-spread` from the network spread, `tally` from the ledger. Duplicate and edit.
5. **Press K before exporting** (or run `tools/check.py`) and clear every editorial error: missing source tags, missing DRAMATISATION or RECREATION labels.
6. **Check stills, not playback.** Run `tools/stills.py` and look at the frames (the contact sheet makes this fast). It's quicker than watching every scene.
7. **Export with Shift+E.** Files are named by slug and scene id, e.g. `ep02-switch-explainer.mp4`. Rename with the line number in front (`L15-switch-explainer.mp4`) so they sort in script order in the editor.
8. **Make the cue sheet:** `tools/cues.py` gives you a CSV with the exact second of every graphic hit (pin pops, highlights, stamps). Use it to place sound effects in seconds, not by ear. Remember each file has 0.25 s of black at the start.

**One-offs to build** (not in the toolkit yet): the stopwatch (L05, L37, L45), the two-server icon (L08), the clapperboard (L10, L31), and the icon multiplication grid (L25). The stopwatch is worth adding to the toolkit properly, since time-pressure stories will need it again.

## C14. Blender workflow

- **Render only after the radio edit is locked**, so every shot is exactly the right length the first time.
- **Make an animatic first:** grab viewport screenshots (or render at 25% resolution, 1 sample) and drop them on the timeline. It costs an hour and tells you whether each shot works before you spend render time.
- **Render settings for speed:** Eevee, 1920×1080, **30 fps**, motion blur on, soft shadows on, 32–64 samples. Cycles only if a shot really needs it.
- **Render to PNG sequences**, not MP4. If Blender crashes at frame 400, you keep frames 1–399.
- **Batch render overnight** from the command line, one `.blend` after another:
  `blender -b ep02-atm-booth.blend -a`
- **Name renders by line:** `L38-kolhapur-cctv/`, `L48-swift-envelopes/`.
- **One rig, recoloured,** as in EP01. Simple walk, stand, hand-over and look animations, reused.
- Add the **DRAMATISATION** label in the editor as a single overlay clip on its own video track, across every ANIM shot, so you can't forget one.

## C15. Editing fast

### Set up once, reuse every episode

- **Make a template project.** Duplicate the EP01 project, delete all media and clips, and keep the tracks, buses, bins, adjustment layers and export preset. Save it as `Unfiction-Template`. Every episode starts from it.
- **Track layout:**
  - V1 for `ME` footage
  - V2 for GFX and ANIM
  - V3 for overlays (DRAMATISATION label, punch-in titles)
  - V4 for captions (Shorts only)
  - A1–A9 for audio, as in the sound plan
- **Folder structure on disk:**

```text
EP02-cosmos-bank-heist/
  01-script/        this file, VO script, camera script
  02-audio/raw/     original recordings
  02-audio/clean/   Voice Studio output
  03-camera/        ME footage
  04-gfx/           toolkit exports, named L15-...
  05-blender/       renders, named L38-...
  06-music/         music cues M01–M09
  07-sfx/           sound effects
  08-exports/       final video, Shorts, thumbnail
  sound-log.csv
```

### The editing order

Do the edit in passes, each one complete before the next. Jumping between picture, sound and colour is what makes editing take forever.

1. **Radio edit (sound only).** Lay the cleaned VO and `ME` audio in script order. Remove bad takes, breaths and long gaps. Leave the planned silences. Watch nothing, just listen. **This timeline is now locked.** Add a marker at the start of every line (L01, L02…).
2. **`ME` picture.** Put the chosen `ME` takes on V1, synced to their audio. Add the CU punch-ins on hook lines.
3. **Graphics.** Drop each `L..-name.mp4` on V2 at its marker. Because the beats were built from the radio edit, they line up without trimming.
4. **Blender.** Same: drop each render at its marker.
5. **Gaps and pacing.** Watch through once. Anywhere the picture stays the same for more than about 6 seconds, add a crop, a punch-in or a cutaway.
6. **Sound.** Music cues by act first, then SFX at the cue-sheet times, then ducking, then loudness.
7. **Colour.** One grade on an adjustment layer over all `ME` footage. Graphics and renders don't need grading.
8. **Captions.** Auto-transcribe, then correct names: Cosmos, Kolhapur, RuPay, Visa, SWIFT, Hang Seng, ALM Trading, Alaumary, Brijesh Singh, Intel 471, FASTCash.

### Time-saving tricks

- **Text-based editing.** DaVinci Resolve and Premiere Pro both let you edit from a transcript. Because you said "L08 take 2" before each take, you can search for "L08", see both takes as text, and delete the bad one by deleting its words.
- **J-cuts and L-cuts.** Let the next line's audio start a few frames before its picture (J-cut), or the picture change before the voice ends (L-cut). The edit feels smooth and nobody notices the cuts.
- **Learn ten shortcuts** in your editor: blade/cut, ripple delete, J/K/L playback, add marker, next/previous edit, zoom timeline, snap on/off, link/unlink audio, match frame, render preview.
- **Proxies.** If 4K HEVC stutters on your PC, generate proxies (half resolution, ProRes or DNxHR) and edit with them. Switch back to the originals only to export.
- **Don't polish while assembling.** Get the whole video rough first, then polish. Perfecting the first minute while the rest is empty is the most common time sink.
- **Copy whole sequences between episodes.** The logo sting, end screen, DRAMATISATION overlay and lower-third style should be in the template.

### Review passes (in this order)

1. **Story pass:** watch the whole video once without stopping. Write down timecodes where your attention drops. Fix those.
2. **Silent pass:** watch with the sound off. Does every line's visual match what it says (the say-it-show-it rule)? Is anything on screen too long?
3. **Audio pass:** listen with your eyes closed, on headphones. Pops, clicks, music too loud, uneven voice levels.
4. **Phone pass:** watch the whole export on your phone, as most viewers will. Is the on-screen text readable? Are the source tags legible?
5. **Fact pass:** read the "Points where sources disagree" list again, and check every number on screen against the script.

### Export settings

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Format | MP4, H.264 |
| Resolution | **2560×1440 or 3840×2160**, even from a 1080p timeline. YouTube gives higher-resolution uploads a better codec and bitrate, so the video looks sharper even when viewed at 1080p. |
| Frame rate | 30 fps |
| Bitrate | 1440p: about 24 Mbps · 4K: about 45 Mbps · 1080p: about 16 Mbps |
| Audio | AAC, 320 kbps, 48 kHz, stereo |
| Loudness | −14 LUFS integrated, −1 dBTP true peak |
| Colour | Rec.709, SDR |
